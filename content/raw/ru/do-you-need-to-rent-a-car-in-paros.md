## The Short Answer: Do You Need a Car in Paros?

You do not **need** a car to visit Paros, but you will get far more out of the island with one. If you are staying for 2 nights in Naoussa or Parikia and only want the main town and one nearby beach, public buses and the occasional taxi are enough. If you are staying 3+ days, travelling as a couple or family, or you want to chase the best beaches, hilltop villages and sunset tavernas on your own schedule, renting a car is well worth it, and often cheaper than repeated taxi fares.

## Getting Around Paros Without a Car

### Public buses (KTEL)

Paros has a decent bus network connecting Parikia (the main port), Naoussa, Pounta, Aliki, Lefkes and the popular Golden Beach. Buses are cheap (around €2, €3 per ride) and reliable in summer, but they run on fixed timetables, get crowded in August, and stop early in the evening. Reaching quieter beaches and inland villages by bus is slow or simply not possible.

### Taxis

Paros has a limited number of taxis, so in peak season you may wait a long time or struggle to find one late at night. Fares add up quickly, a few cross-island trips can cost more than a full day of car hire. Pre-booking a transfer is smart for airport or port arrivals, but taxis are not an efficient way to explore.

### Scooters, ATVs and bikes

Two wheels are fun and easy to park. A [scooter rental](https://antiparosrentacar.com/ru/%d0%b0%d1%80%d0%b5%d0%bd%d0%b4%d0%b0-%d1%81%d0%ba%d1%83%d1%82%d0%b5%d1%80%d0%be%d0%b2-%d0%bd%d0%b0-%d0%b0%d0%bd%d1%82%d0%b8%d0%bf%d0%b0%d1%80%d0%be%d1%81%d0%b5/) or an [ATV / quad bike](https://antiparosrentacar.com/ru/%d0%ba%d0%b2%d0%b0%d0%b4%d1%80%d0%be%d1%86%d0%b8%d0%ba%d0%bb%d1%8b-%d0%b0%d0%bd%d1%82%d0%b8%d0%bf%d0%b0%d1%80%d0%be%d1%81/) is perfect for short hops and solo travellers, while [bicycles](https://antiparosrentacar.com/ru/%d0%b2%d0%b5%d0%bb%d0%be%d1%81%d0%b8%d0%bf%d0%b5%d0%b4%d0%bd%d1%8b%d0%b9-%d0%bf%d0%b0%d1%80%d0%ba/) suit the flat coastal stretches. The downside: limited luggage space, less comfort over distance, and exposure to wind and midday heat. For families or longer drives, a car wins.

## Driving in Paros: Roads, Parking and Tips

- Roads between main towns are paved and in good condition; some beach access tracks are unpaved, drive slowly.
- Parking in Parikia and Naoussa fills up fast in summer; arrive early or park on the edge and walk in.
- Fuel stations are mainly near Parikia and Naoussa, so top up before long drives.
- Greeks drive on the right. An EU licence is fine; non-EU visitors should carry an International Driving Permit.
- Watch for scooters, pedestrians and the occasional goat on rural roads!
