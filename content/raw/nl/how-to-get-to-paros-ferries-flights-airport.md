## Where Is Paros?

Paros lies in the central Aegean, roughly 160 km southeast of Athens, between Mykonos to the north and Santorini to the south, and just across a narrow channel from Naxos. Its central location makes it a natural hub for Cyclades island hopping.

## How to Get to Paros by Ferry

### From Athens (Piraeus and Rafina)

Most travellers reach Paros by ferry from **Piraeus**, the main port of Athens. High-speed ferries take roughly 3 to 3.5 hours, while conventional (slower, cheaper, steadier) ferries take about 4 to 5 hours. Ferries also depart from **Rafina** port, which is closer to Athens International Airport. Book early for July and August, when sailings sell out.

### From other islands

- Mykonos to Paros: about 45 minutes by high-speed ferry.
- Naxos to Paros: roughly 30, 45 minutes, the shortest hop.
- Santorini to Paros: about 2 to 3 hours depending on the boat.
- Ios to Paros: around 1.5 to 2 hours.

## How to Get to Paros by Plane

Paros National Airport (airport code **PAS**) sits near Alyki in the south of the island. There are frequent short domestic flights from Athens (about 35, 40 minutes), with extra seasonal routes in summer. Flying saves time but books up fast, so reserve ahead. There are currently no direct international flights to Paros, most visitors fly into Athens and connect by plane or ferry.

**How do you get to Paros from Athens?**

The most common way is the ferry from Piraeus (about 3 to 5 hours) or Rafina port. You can also fly from Athens to Paros National Airport in roughly 35 to 40 minutes.

**Does Paros have an airport?**

Yes. Paros National Airport (PAS) is near Alyki in the south of the island and handles frequent domestic flights from Athens, plus extra seasonal routes. There are no direct international flights.

**How long is the ferry from Athens to Paros?**

High-speed ferries take about 3 to 3.5 hours from Piraeus, while conventional ferries take roughly 4 to 5 hours.

**How do you get from Mykonos or Santorini to Paros?**
